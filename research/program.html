RESEARCH AREA 01 · ORIGIN OF LIFE

Research programme

Two directions on one question: under what conditions could living chemistry begin?

The question

How could chemistry on early Earth move from simple molecules to systems that hold reactions, copy information and divide? This programme approaches the question from two sides. One starts from the geochemical setting, the other from a computational model of compartments. The aim is to say what has been shown, what is being tested and what is still missing, without treating any part as settled.

Two directions

A · Geochemical scenario

Mineral-rich water as the setting for the first reactions. The working case is evaporating alkaline lakes, which concentrate minerals and chemicals. Hydrothermal vents are kept as a comparison setting.

Open questions: water chemistry in lake-like settings, mineral energy sources such as serpentinisation, and whether short peptides form and persist under those conditions.

B · Computational protocell model

A stochastic model of compartment populations with internal reaction networks, division, inheritance and a shared resource pool. It explores which conditions let compartments persist and grow.

Open questions: the statistics need to be redone, the rules need auditing, and the assumed rate constants need linking to measured values where they exist.

The two directions meet at one point: the model needs chemistry that the geochemical scenario can supply, and the geochemistry needs a way to test whether compartments can hold that chemistry.

What is not yet claimed

  • The protocell model is a simplified computational framework. It does not show a historical pathway for the origin of life.
  • The coacervate illustration is not evidence that coacervates played a role in the origin of life.
  • No laboratory experiment in this programme has been run yet.
  • The geochemical scenario is a hypothesis, not a result.
