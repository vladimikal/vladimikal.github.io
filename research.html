My research explores questions across biological organisation, physical theory and the history of life. I approach these subjects through computational modelling, theoretical analysis, comparative biology and open scientific work.

01 / ORIGIN OF LIFE

Protocell Dynamics

Computational model

How can simple chemical compartments develop persistent organisation? What roles can catalysis, resource availability, internal metabolism and inheritance play in the dynamics of primitive cell-like systems?

This project uses an individual-based computational model of protocell populations. The model explores interactions between molecular components, catalytic processes, resource uptake, enzyme production, growth, division and the inheritance of model parameters.

Methods

C++ simulation, stochastic reaction modelling, population-level analysis, repeated simulation runs and comparison of model conditions.

Research questions

Under what conditions can catalytic activity affect population dynamics? What is the role of translation, compartmentalisation and inheritance in the model?

The model is an exploratory theoretical system. Its assumptions and limitations must be considered when interpreting simulation outcomes.

02 / QUANTUM FOUNDATIONS

Quantum Measurement

Theoretical research

This direction examines the conceptual foundations of quantum mechanics, with particular attention to ensemble interpretations, the physical meaning of measurement and the relationship between quantum systems and their environments.

A central historical and conceptual reference is D. I. Blokhintsev's approach to quantum theory. The research also considers the relationship between ensemble descriptions and decoherence, including questions about whether measurement can be understood without introducing a conscious observer.

Methods

Conceptual analysis, historical comparison of interpretations, examination of foundational literature and theoretical argumentation.

Research questions

What is the physical status of the quantum ensemble? How should measurement be understood in an observer-independent physical description?

A preliminary manuscript on these questions is available through Zenodo. It should be treated as a preprint, not as a final peer-reviewed publication.

03 / PALEOSOUND

Insect Acoustics

Research project

PaleoSound explores the relationship between biological structure and sound production, with a particular research interest in grasshoppers and their acoustic mechanisms.

The project brings together comparative morphology, bioacoustics, fossil evidence and computational approaches to investigate how anatomical structures can inform hypotheses about sound production in organisms, including those from the distant past.

Methods

Morphological comparison, literature-based evidence gathering, acoustic analysis and computational visualisation.

Research questions

How do anatomical structures relate to sound production? What can comparative biology tell us about the acoustic capabilities of extinct organisms?

Reconstructions and acoustic hypotheses require explicit treatment of uncertainty. Visualisations are not substitutes for fossil measurements or acoustic data.

Research principles

Evidence before appearance.

I aim to distinguish published findings, direct observations, model outputs, working hypotheses and conceptual illustrations. Computational results are interpreted in the context of model assumptions and limitations. Uncertainty is part of the research process and should be made visible.

Wherever possible, research materials, code and scientific explanations will be made publicly accessible.

Support independent research

Help sustain open science.

Contributions help support computing, research materials, data analysis and the preparation of public research outputs. Core research materials will remain openly accessible.
